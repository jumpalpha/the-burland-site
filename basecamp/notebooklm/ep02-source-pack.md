## About this episode

Episode 2 is a practical guide for a family reunion group doing Yellowstone in a single day. The audience is a mix of ages: grandparents, parents, and kids of varying ages. The tone is warm and realistic. The goal is to help them have a great day without getting caught out by crowds, distance, or a tired four-year-old at 3 p.m.

The main things to cover:
1. Whether one day is enough, and when it makes sense
2. What time to leave and why
3. Which two or three stops are worth it for mixed-age groups
4. How to handle parking, crowds, and the heat of the day
5. Getting home in time for dinner

## About The Burland (brief reminder)

The Burland is a restored 1912 building in St. Anthony, Idaho, used for family reunions, weddings, and group stays. This show is built around the idea that The Burland is the basecamp: the home you come back to after a full day out. That framing applies perfectly to a Yellowstone day trip.

## Is one day enough?

One day is enough for a first visit or for a family with young kids. You will not see everything, and that is fine. The goal for a one-day trip with grandkids is to see two or three iconic things well, eat lunch without stress, and get everyone back without anyone melting down.

The two stops that work best for all ages are **Grand Prismatic Spring** and **Old Faithful**. Both have flat, paved boardwalks. Both are genuinely spectacular. Both can be done in a morning and an early afternoon.

If the group has teenagers or stronger hikers, the Fairy Falls overlook trail gives a view down onto Grand Prismatic from above -- it's a moderate, mostly flat trail of about five miles round trip and well worth the extra time.

---

## Timing: why leaving early matters

The West Entrance, through the town of West Yellowstone, is the closest gateway from St. Anthony. It is also the most popular entrance in summer. Parking at Grand Prismatic Spring and the Midway Geyser Basin area fills up early. By mid-morning, the overflow lots are full and cars are lined up along the road.

**Recommendation:** leave before 7:00 a.m. Arrive at Grand Prismatic before 9:00 a.m. and you will park easily and walk with almost no crowd. Wait until 10:30 and you may park more than a mile away and walk in traffic.

Packing a full breakfast or eating before you leave is better than stopping in West Yellowstone on the way in. Save the town for dinner on the way home, when the traffic has thinned.

## Stop 1: Grand Prismatic Spring and Midway Geyser Basin

**What it is:** The largest hot spring in the United States, famous for its concentric rings of color -- deep blue center, then green, yellow, orange, and rust, caused by heat-loving bacteria at different temperatures. It is roughly 300 feet across.

**The boardwalk:** Flat, paved, and stroller and wheelchair accessible. The main loop around the spring takes about 20 to 30 minutes at a relaxed pace. It is exposed -- hot, with steam depending on wind direction -- so hats, sunscreen, and water bottles matter.

**The Fairy Falls overlook:** A separate trailhead, about a half mile from the main Midway parking area, leads to Fairy Falls (a tall, narrow waterfall) and continues to an overlook ridge above Grand Prismatic. The overlook is about 2.5 miles each way from the trailhead. For a family with grandparents or small kids, the overlook is optional. For a group with teens or active adults, it is worth it and adds a couple of hours.

**Practical notes:**
- Arrive before 9:00 a.m. to find parking in the main lot.
- There are restrooms at the trailhead.
- Do not get off the boardwalk or touch the water. The hot springs are scalding and the ground crust is thin.

---

## Stop 2: Old Faithful and the Upper Geyser Basin

**What it is:** The most famous geyser in the world, erupting roughly every 60 to 110 minutes to a height of 100 to 180 feet, for 1.5 to 5 minutes. The Old Faithful Visitor Education Center posts the predicted time for the next eruption, accurate to within about 10 minutes.

**The boardwalk:** A wide, flat paved path encircles the geyser. Benches and viewing areas fill up 10 to 15 minutes before an eruption. For grandparents and kids who are done walking, this is a great stop -- sit down, wait, watch, and go.

**The Upper Geyser Basin walk:** A five-mile loop of the Upper Geyser Basin runs past a dozen more geysers, including Castle, Riverside, and Morning Glory Pool. Families with energy and time can walk it. Families with smaller kids can do the half-mile loop closest to Old Faithful and still see several active features.

**Lunch:** Old Faithful Inn, the historic log hotel, has a dining room and a deli. The deli is faster and works for a midday stop. Expect a wait at the dining room. Packing lunch is simpler if the group is large.

**Practical notes:**
- Parking here is extensive but fills by mid-morning in peak summer. Arriving before 11:00 a.m. is easier.
- The visitor center is worth a few minutes for the eruption prediction and the exhibits on how geysers work.
- The area is at about 7,300 feet elevation. Heat plus altitude plus walking adds up. Breaks and water matter.

## West Yellowstone for dinner (optional)

The town of West Yellowstone sits just outside the park's west boundary. It has restaurants, gas stations, and a good ice cream stop. After a full day in the park, most families find that dinner in West Yellowstone and the drive home feels like the right ending. The town is small and tourist-focused, but it works.

The **Grizzly and Wolf Discovery Center** is in West Yellowstone, right next to the park entrance. It is an educational nonprofit with large enclosures where grizzly bears and wolves live year-round. If the park did not deliver an animal sighting, this is the guaranteed backup. Plan 45 minutes to an hour.

## What to pack

For a mixed-age group, day-trip basics:
- Water bottles (one per person, more than you think you need)
- Snacks and a packed lunch, or plan the deli stop
- Sunscreen and hats -- the geyser basins are exposed
- An extra layer -- mornings can be cool even in July
- Comfortable walking shoes with closed toes
- A stroller for little kids; the boardwalks are paved
- Phone chargers -- navigation uses battery

---

## Park access and fees

As of 2026, the standard park entry fee is $35 per vehicle for a seven-day pass. An America the Beautiful Annual Pass ($80 at time of writing) covers entry to all national parks and pays for itself quickly if the family plans more than two or three national park visits in a year.

The park is open year-round. Most interior roads, including the roads to the geyser basins, are open to cars from mid-April through the end of October. Exact dates vary by year and conditions -- check the National Park Service website before you go.

## For grandparents and kids with mobility needs

Both Grand Prismatic Spring and Old Faithful have the flattest, most accessible boardwalks in the park. Neither requires stairs or uneven terrain. The restroom facilities at both are accessible. The main challenge at both is heat and sun exposure in midday.

If the group has members who do better with short walks and long sits, the formula works: walk Grand Prismatic in the morning (30 minutes), drive to Old Faithful, wait and watch the eruption (20 minutes of standing, then sit and watch), have lunch, and head home.

## A sample day timeline

This is an example. Groups should adjust to their own pace.

- 6:30 a.m. -- Leave The Burland, packed breakfast or stop on the road
- Arrive at Midway Geyser Basin / Grand Prismatic -- walk the boardwalk before the crowds arrive
- Optional: Fairy Falls overlook trail for the group members who want more
- Drive to Old Faithful
- Check the visitor center for the next eruption time
- Watch Old Faithful erupt
- Lunch at the deli or packed lunch at the picnic area
- Upper Geyser Basin walk, or rest and head home
- Drive back through West Yellowstone -- dinner and optional Grizzly and Wolf Discovery Center
- Home by 7:00 or 8:00 p.m.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Leaving too late.** Parking and crowds compound from about 10:00 a.m. onward at both main stops.
- **Skipping the eruption prediction.** Check the board in the visitor center. Waiting 20 minutes for a predicted eruption beats waiting 90 minutes without one.
- **Too many stops.** One or two stops done well beats five stops done in a rush with tired kids. Keep it to Grand Prismatic and Old Faithful.
- **Underestimating the heat and altitude.** The geyser basins are sunny, exposed, and at elevation. Sun and dehydration hit faster than expected.
- **No bathroom plan.** There are restrooms at both main stops and at the visitor centers. Identify them when you arrive.

## About The Burland

The Burland is a restored building in downtown St. Anthony, Idaho. It was built in 1912. Restoration started in 2020 and went from the roof down: a new roof, new wood floors, new ceilings, and the original character kept wherever it could be saved.

Today it is a place for family reunions, weddings, and gatherings where everyone stays under one roof. It has over 10,000 square feet on three floors and sleeps 40 or more guests. Families can rent the whole building or just the spaces they need.

### The rooms

Shared spaces:
- **Aspen Ballroom**: dinners, dancing, programs, and the big family photo.
- **Game Room**: ping-pong, air hockey, and an arcade corner for kids and teens.
- **Movie / Play Room**: movie nights and a place for the little ones to wind down.
- **Kitchen**: a full kitchen built for cooking for a crowd.

The named rooms (names only, details coming in a later episode): Bear Den, the Loft, Whitetail Room, Buffalo Herd, Wolf Den, Moose Crossing, and Fishing Pond.

### Why "basecamp"

The idea of this show: The Burland is the home base. A good reunion week mixes a couple of big day trips with easy days close to home, and evenings back at the building for meals, games, and time together. Over the coming episodes, the show goes deep on one part of the area at a time.

## The area at a glance

St. Anthony is in Eastern Idaho, in the Upper Snake River Valley. Within reach are Yellowstone and Grand Teton National Parks, the Henry's Fork and South Fork of the Snake River, the Island Park and Ashton country, the lava fields of the Snake River Plain, and the towns of Rexburg, Idaho Falls, Driggs, Victor, and Jackson, Wyoming.

Everything in the Burland Basecamp guide is within 150 miles of The Burland.

### Close to home

- **St. Anthony Sand Dunes** (local favorite). About 10,600 acres of open white quartz sand, run by the Bureau of Land Management. People ride ATVs and dirt bikes, sandboard, and let kids run. Some dunes are hundreds of feet tall. Egin Lakes, at the edge of the dunes, has a campground and a spot to cool off. Parts of the area around the dunes close each winter into spring for wildlife, so check with the BLM before a winter or early-spring visit.
- **Rexburg**. The nearest bigger town: groceries, restaurants, BYU-Idaho, and two temples. Porter Park has a restored antique carousel for little kids. The Teton Flood Museum tells the story of the 1976 Teton Dam collapse that flooded this valley, a good rainy-day stop.
- **Yellowstone Bear World**. A drive-through wildlife park near Rexburg with bears, elk, and bison.
- **Menan Buttes** (local favorite). Volcanic cones rising out of the farmland, with a short, steep hike to the crater rim.
- **Ashton-Tetonia Rail Trail**. A rail trail through farm country with old trestle bridges and Teton views.
- **Green Canyon Hot Springs** (local favorite). A family-run hot pool in a quiet canyon.

### The Ashton and Island Park side

- **Upper and Lower Mesa Falls** (local favorite). On the Henry's Fork, along the Mesa Falls Scenic Byway. Upper Mesa Falls drops about 114 feet and has an accessible boardwalk from the visitor center. Lower Mesa Falls, about 65 feet, has its own overlook.
- **Warm River** (local favorite). A slow, clear river for tubing and picnics, with a fish-feeding spot. An easy stop on the same trip as Mesa Falls.
- **Harriman State Park**. A historic ranch on the Henry's Fork, with trumpeter swans, moose, easy trails, and famous fly-fishing water.
- **Cave Falls** (local favorite). A wide, low waterfall in Yellowstone's quiet southwest corner, the Bechler area, reached from Ashton without going through a main park entrance. The last part of the road is gravel and can be rough.

### Full-day trips

- **Yellowstone National Park through West Yellowstone**. The West Entrance is the closest full-service gateway, and the road leads to the geyser basins: Grand Prismatic Spring, the rainbow-colored hot spring, and Old Faithful with its flat boardwalks. Leave early. Parking at Grand Prismatic fills by mid-morning. In West Yellowstone, the Grizzly & Wolf Discovery Center guarantees a bear and wolf sighting even if the park doesn't.
- **Yellowstone by season**. Most park roads are open to cars from mid-April through the end of October. In 2026, the National Park Service's projected dates for the West Entrance roads were April 17 to October 31. In winter the interior is over-snow travel only, by guided snowmobile or snowcoach, starting mid-December. Island Park and West Yellowstone have hundreds of miles of groomed snowmobile trails.
- **Grand Teton National Park and Jackson**. Jenny Lake, its shuttle boat, and easy lakeshore walks. Jackson has the antler-arch town square and, in winter, sleigh rides on the National Elk Refuge. On the way, Driggs and Victor in Teton Valley (local favorite) are small towns on the Idaho side of the Tetons, near Grand Targhee Resort.
- **Craters of the Moon and lava country**. Lava flows, cinder cones, and lava tube caves you can walk into. Bring flashlights, closed-toe shoes, and water. On the way is EBR-I, where electricity was first produced from nuclear power in 1951.

### Rivers and fishing (a preview of Episode 3)

The Henry's Fork, including Box Canyon and Last Chance, is famous fly-fishing water. The South Fork of the Snake through Swan Valley is known for float trips, bald eagles, and cutthroat trout. Island Park Reservoir and Henry's Lake are big trout lakes.

### Winter close to home

Kelly Canyon (local favorite) is a small, affordable local ski hill with night skiing. Heise Hot Springs has hot pools by the Snake River.

## Three ready-made reunion days

1. **Yellowstone in a day.** Leave early with packed lunches, go through Island Park to West Yellowstone, see Grand Prismatic Spring before the parking fills, then Old Faithful and the boardwalks, and dinner in West Yellowstone before heading home. Summer and early fall only.
2. **Dunes, falls, and a river.** Morning at the sand dunes while the sand is cool, a picnic at Warm River, Upper and Lower Mesa Falls in the afternoon, then back to The Burland for dinner and the game room.
3. **Easy day, rain or shine.** Yellowstone Bear World in the morning, lunch in Rexburg and the Porter Park carousel, the Teton Flood Museum if it rains or Menan Buttes if it doesn't, then Green Canyon Hot Springs and a movie night.

## How to plan the week

- Pick one or two big days (Yellowstone, the Tetons, or Craters of the Moon) and spread them out.
- Fill the other days with close-to-home trips: the dunes, Mesa Falls and Warm River, Rexburg.
- Leave evenings for the ballroom, the kitchen, and the game room.
- Grandparents and little kids do best with short drives, boardwalks, and a bathroom plan.
- Always check park, road, and weather conditions before heading out, especially outside summer.

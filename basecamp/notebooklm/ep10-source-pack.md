## When to go: plan it now for next fall

This episode is released in late winter. That makes it a planning episode. Fall in Eastern Idaho runs roughly from September into early November, and it changes fast. Early fall can feel like summer. By late October, snow is possible in the mountains.

- **Yellowstone:** roads from the West Entrance are generally open to cars into early November. Check the National Park Service for exact closing dates.
- **Grand Teton National Park:** campgrounds, lodges, and some visitor centers begin closing or cutting hours in September. The main park road closes to cars on November 1.
- **Mesa Falls visitor center (Big Falls Inn):** open roughly mid-May to early October.
- **St. Anthony Sand Dunes:** open in fall. The big dunes close from January 1 to April 1 for wintering wildlife.
- **Fall colors:** generally late September into early October, but the timing changes every year.
- **Hunting seasons:** set by Idaho Fish and Game each year. Licenses, tags, and any drawings happen well before fall, so hunters should start planning now.

Always tell listeners to check current conditions, seasons, and regulations before they go.

## Why fall is the quiet season

Summer is high season in this region, built around school vacations and Yellowstone's peak months. Once school starts again, visitation drops, and by mid to late September the parks and towns feel noticeably calmer. Some park services begin to close or cut hours as visitation falls and the weather changes.

For the right family, that is the appeal: fewer people at the viewpoints, easier parking, crisp mornings, golden afternoons, and wildlife that is especially active. The tradeoff is that some services are closed and the weather is less predictable. Pack layers, and check what is open before you go.

## Hunting

Eastern Idaho is hunting country. The Caribou-Targhee National Forest, which covers the mountains around Island Park and Ashton, has elk, deer, moose, and upland birds.

### What hunters should know

- **Everything comes from Idaho Fish and Game.** Licenses, tags, seasons, hunting units, and rules are all set by Idaho Fish and Game. Some hunts are general hunts, and some are controlled hunts filled through a drawing. Nonresidents have their own license and tag rules.
- **Hunter education.** Idaho has hunter education requirements. Check Idaho Fish and Game to see what applies to each person in the party, especially younger hunters.
- **Plan early.** Licenses, tags, and drawings happen well before the season starts. A hunting trip for next fall should be planned this winter and spring.
- **Follow the rules exactly.** Regulations change from year to year. Every hunter in the group is responsible for reading the current Idaho Fish and Game rules for the place and species they are hunting.
- **Safety and courtesy.** Many hunters wear blaze orange so others can see them. Know where public land ends and private land begins, and get permission before hunting private land.

### Why The Burland works for a hunting group

A hunting trip often brings a group of family or friends together for several days. The Burland sleeps 40 or more in private family suites, so a large hunting party, or hunters plus the rest of the family, can stay under one roof. The full kitchen is set up for big-group cooking, which makes early breakfasts and big dinners at the end of the day easy. The ballroom's long tables are where the day's stories get told.

## Fall fishing

Fall is one of the best times of year to fish in Eastern Idaho, and one of the least crowded.

**The Henry's Fork.** The Henry's Fork runs right through St. Anthony and is one of the West's famous trout rivers. Upstream, the Railroad Ranch water at Harriman State Park is famous dry-fly water. In fall, cooler nights bring more regular hatches, and brown trout take on their deep fall colors. Later in the fall, brown trout spawn, and anglers should take care not to walk on spawning beds in the gravel.

**Other waters.** The South Fork of the Snake, Island Park Reservoir, and Henry's Lake all have fall fishing. Some waters have special seasons or rules.

**Before you fish.** Every angler needs an Idaho fishing license, which can be bought online. Seasons and rules vary by water, and some sections close in late fall. Check the current Idaho Fish and Game regulations for each water before going. Local fly shops can help with conditions.

## Fall colors

In fall, the aspens in the mountains turn bright yellow and gold, and the cottonwoods along the rivers turn too. The peak usually comes in late September into early October, but it changes every year with the weather.

Good places to see fall color:

- **The Mesa Falls Scenic Byway**, from Ashton through the Caribou-Targhee National Forest to Upper and Lower Mesa Falls. Fall is a beautiful time for this drive, and the falls are still roaring. The Big Falls Inn visitor center is generally open into early October.
- **Teton Valley**, on the Idaho side of the Tetons, where the mountains rise above aspen-covered slopes and farm fields.
- **Grand Teton National Park**, where the aspens turn mostly yellow, with less common oranges and reds, below the peaks.
- **The Henry's Fork in town**, where an evening walk on the Henry's Fork Greenway in St. Anthony is an easy way to see the cottonwoods change.

## Wildlife in fall

**The elk rut.** Fall is mating season for elk, called the rut, generally from early September to mid-October. Bull elk bugle, a high, eerie call that carries across valleys, and gather groups of cows. In Yellowstone, the Mammoth Hot Springs area is especially known for rut activity. In Grand Teton National Park, early morning and evening are the best times to listen.

**Safety during the rut.** Bull elk are aggressive in fall. Stay at least 25 yards from elk and other large animals, and at least 100 yards from bears and wolves. Use binoculars or a long lens.

**Trumpeter swans.** The Henry's Fork in and around Harriman State Park is a major wintering area for trumpeter swans. Some swans live on the Henry's Fork year-round, and others arrive later in the year to spend the winter.

**Bears in fall.** Bears are actively feeding before winter. On trails, carry bear spray and know how to use it.

## Harvest season in Eastern Idaho

Fall is harvest time on the farms around St. Anthony. Eastern Idaho is potato country, and the farm country around Ashton is one of Idaho's major seed potato growing areas. In some rural school districts in Eastern Idaho, the potato harvest has long been important enough that schools take a "spud break," a pause of a week or two so students can help bring in the crop. Families visiting in early fall may see trucks loaded with potatoes on the roads and harvest equipment in the fields. It is a side of Eastern Idaho that summer visitors never see.

## Building a fall day

**Option A: Fall colors and falls.** North to Ashton and up the Mesa Falls Scenic Byway for the aspens. Upper and Lower Mesa Falls (check that the visitor center is still open). Picnic at Warm River. Back to The Burland for dinner.

**Option B: Elk and geysers.** A Yellowstone day in late September or early October, with smaller crowds than summer and a chance to hear elk bugling. Leave early, pack layers and lunches, and check road status before going.

**Option C: The angler's day.** A day on the Henry's Fork, at Harriman, or on the South Fork, with Idaho fishing licenses in hand and current rules checked. Non-anglers can walk the trails at Harriman or explore Island Park.

**Option D: The hunting party.** Early breakfast at The Burland, a long day in the field following Idaho Fish and Game rules, and a big dinner around the ballroom tables in the evening.

**Option E: The slow fall day.** A walk on the Henry's Fork Greenway, the St. Anthony Sand Dunes on a cool afternoon, and an evening at the hot springs.

## A note on what guests expect

Most families think of Eastern Idaho as a summer destination. Hunters already know better, and so do anglers. For everyone else, fall is a discovery: quieter parks, cool mornings, gold aspens, and the sound of elk in the evening. It is not the season for every family. Some services close, and the weather can turn. But for groups who can travel outside summer vacation, fall may be the best-kept secret of the year.

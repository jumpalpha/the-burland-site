## About this episode

Episode 6 is the backup-plan episode. Every reunion has a day when the weather does not cooperate: a summer thunderstorm, a cold rain, or a winter day too bitter for the snow. Kids still need somewhere to put their energy, and grandparents still want something to do. This episode gives families a list of indoor and weather-proof options in Eastern Idaho, starting with the ones under The Burland's own roof.

The episode covers:
1. Start at home: The Burland's game room, media room, and gathering spaces
2. Museums with real local and national stories: the Teton Flood Museum and the Museum of Idaho
3. Animals rain or shine: the Grizzly and Wolf Discovery Center and Yellowstone Bear World
4. Warm water: Heise and Green Canyon hot springs
5. Summer extras for a rainy day: the Porter Park carousel and the Idaho Falls Zoo
6. Which places work in winter and which are summer only
7. How to build a rainy day that works for mixed ages

## When to go: what is open now, and what to save for summer

This episode is released in the middle of winter. Some of these places are open all year, so they work for a winter gathering right now. Others are summer-only, and they are worth remembering for a rainy day on a summer reunion.

- **Year-round:** The Burland's own game room and media room, the Museum of Idaho, the Grizzly and Wolf Discovery Center, and the hot pools at Heise Hot Springs. Green Canyon Hot Springs is also open in winter, but with more limited hours. The Teton Flood Museum keeps limited hours that can depend on volunteers, so call ahead.
- **Summer season:** Yellowstone Bear World, the Porter Park carousel, and the Idaho Falls Zoo all operate seasonally, roughly spring or early summer into fall.

Always tell listeners to check current hours and seasons before they go.

## About The Burland (brief reminder)

The Burland is a restored 1912 building in downtown St. Anthony, Idaho, used for family reunions, weddings, and group stays. It sleeps 40 or more people overnight in private family suites. It is designed for all ages, and it has spaces built for exactly the kind of day this episode is about.

## Start at home: rainy days inside The Burland

The easiest rainy-day plan is to stay put. The Burland has room for a big group to spend a whole day indoors without anyone feeling cooped up.

**The game room.** Kids and teens get their own game room, with a ping-pong table, an air hockey table, and an arcade corner. On a rainy day, this is where the cousins disappear to.

**The media room.** The media room is set up for movie night, and a rainy afternoon is a good excuse for a matinee.

**The upstairs lounge.** The upstairs lounge has sectionals overlooking the ballroom. It is a good spot for the adults to talk, read, or keep an eye on things.

**The kitchen and ballroom.** The full kitchen is set up for big-group cooking, with a large island and a coffee bar. A rainy day is a good day for a big cooking project: a family recipe, a baking contest, or a pancake breakfast that turns into lunch. The ballroom's long tables work for board games, puzzles, and craft projects as well as dinner.

A rainy morning at The Burland, followed by one outing in the afternoon, is often the right pace for a family group.

## Museums

### Teton Flood Museum, Rexburg

On June 5, 1976, the Teton Dam on the Teton River failed. The flood swept through the towns and farms of this valley, including Rexburg and the surrounding communities. It is one of the defining events in the history of Eastern Idaho.

The Museum of Rexburg is home to the Teton Flood exhibit, which tells the story of the dam, the flood, and how the valley recovered. It is real local history, close to home, and it gives kids and adults a new way to see the farmland they drive through.

Practical note: the museum's location and hours have changed in recent years, and hours can depend on volunteers. Call or check the City of Rexburg's museum page before going.

### Museum of Idaho, Idaho Falls

The Museum of Idaho is a history and science museum in downtown Idaho Falls. It brings in major traveling exhibitions, usually one to three a year, on a wide range of subjects, alongside its permanent Idaho exhibit, called "Way Out West." Because the special exhibits rotate, what is showing during any visit will be different. Check the museum's current exhibit list before planning a trip.

For a mixed-age group on a rainy day, this is one of the most reliable options in the region. It is in Idaho Falls, so it pairs well with lunch in town or a stop on the way to or from the Idaho Falls airport.

## Animals, rain or shine

### Grizzly and Wolf Discovery Center, West Yellowstone

The Grizzly and Wolf Discovery Center is a nonprofit wildlife park in West Yellowstone, Montana, near Yellowstone's West Entrance. It is accredited by the Association of Zoos and Aquariums and open every day of the year. Visitors can see grizzly bears and gray wolves up close. Its bears do not hibernate, so they can be seen in winter, too.

It is a long day from The Burland, but it is a guaranteed animal sighting. It works on a Yellowstone day when the weather turns, or as the main event of a winter trip to West Yellowstone.

### Yellowstone Bear World, near Rexburg

Yellowstone Bear World is a drive-through wildlife park just south of Rexburg off US-20. Families drive their own cars through the park and see bears, elk, bison, and other animals from their windows, which is why it works on a rainy day: everyone stays dry. It also has a petting zoo, kids' rides, and bottle-feeding with bear cubs.

Bear World is a summer-season attraction, and it did not open for its most recent season. Before planning a visit, check its website to confirm it is open for the season.

## Warm water: hot springs

Hot springs are a classic answer to a cold, gray day in Eastern Idaho.

**Heise Hot Springs**, near Ririe on the Snake River, keeps its hot mineral pools open all year. In summer it also has a swimming pool with a waterslide, which is summer only.

**Green Canyon Hot Springs** is a family-run hot pool in a quiet canyon. It has outdoor hot pools and a large indoor pool, which makes it a good choice when the weather is bad. Its hours are much more limited outside summer, so call ahead.

## Summer extras for a rainy day

These are summer-season places. They are worth knowing about for a rainy summer reunion day, but they are not winter outings.

**Porter Park and the Idaho Centennial Carousel, Rexburg.** Porter Park is a big, shady city park in Rexburg. Its carousel is an antique wooden carousel built in 1926, the only one of its kind in Idaho. It was badly damaged in the 1976 Teton Dam flood and later restored, reopening around Idaho's centennial in 1990. The carousel is housed in its own building, so it runs in light rain. It operates seasonally, generally from early summer through Labor Day.

**Idaho Falls Zoo at Tautphaus Park.** A walkable zoo, just the right size for small kids, inside a large city park. It operates seasonally, roughly from spring into fall. It is an outdoor zoo, so it is better for a cloudy day or a break between showers than a downpour.

## Building a rainy day

**Option A: The stay-home day (any season).** Pancakes in the morning. Game room tournament: ping-pong and air hockey brackets for the cousins. Big cooking project for lunch. Movie in the media room in the afternoon. Family dinner in the ballroom. No one gets in a car.

**Option B: The history day (year-round).** Morning at the Teton Flood Museum in Rexburg (call ahead for hours). Lunch in Rexburg. Afternoon at the Museum of Idaho in Idaho Falls. Back to The Burland for dinner.

**Option C: The animal day.** In summer: Yellowstone Bear World in the morning, then the Porter Park carousel in Rexburg. In winter or summer: the Grizzly and Wolf Discovery Center in West Yellowstone for a longer day.

**Option D: The warm-up day (year-round).** A slow morning at The Burland, then an afternoon soak at Heise or Green Canyon (check hours), then home for movie night.

## A note on what guests expect

Families planning a reunion around Yellowstone and the outdoors often worry about the weather. A rainy day does not have to be a lost day. The best rainy days at The Burland are usually the ones where nobody tries too hard: a slow morning, a game room tournament, one good outing in the afternoon, and everyone back together for dinner.

## When to plan: now, for summer

This episode is released in the middle of winter. That makes it the right moment to plan. Summer weekends are the first dates to go at The Burland, especially during Yellowstone season and around Pioneer Days in St. Anthony in late July, so families planning a summer reunion should start early.

The sample weeks in this episode are built for summer, roughly June through early September, when Yellowstone's roads, the Tetons, Mesa Falls, lava country, and the summer attractions are all open. Many of those places close or change in the off-season, so always tell listeners to check current seasons before locking in a plan.

## About The Burland (brief reminder)

The Burland is a restored 1912 building in downtown St. Anthony, Idaho, used for family reunions, weddings, and group stays. It sleeps 40 or more people overnight in private family suites, so most families fit under one roof. It has a ballroom that seats 100, a full kitchen set up for big-group cooking, a game room for kids and teens, a media room, and an upstairs lounge. It is designed for all ages. For a reunion week, it is the basecamp everyone comes back to each night.

## Why a reunion week needs a rhythm

The most common mistake in planning a reunion week is trying to do a big day every day. Yellowstone, the Tetons, and Craters of the Moon are all long days from The Burland. Stack them back to back and by midweek the grandparents are exhausted, the toddlers are melting down, and the teenagers are asleep in the car.

A better plan alternates three kinds of days:

- **Big days:** the long trips. Yellowstone, the Tetons and Jackson, Craters of the Moon. Plan no more than two or three in a week, and never two in a row.
- **Close-to-home days:** half-day outings near St. Anthony. The Sand Dunes, the Henry's Fork, Mesa Falls, Menan Buttes, Rexburg.
- **Rest days:** a slow morning, the game room, a long lunch, maybe one short outing in the afternoon. These days are where a lot of the actual reuniting happens.

## The menu of days

Here is a quick recap of what earlier episodes covered. Refer listeners back to those episodes for detail.

**Yellowstone (Episode 2).** The big one. Head north on US-20 through Ashton and Island Park to the West Entrance, then on to the geyser basins, Grand Prismatic Spring, and Old Faithful. A long day. Leave early and pack lunches from The Burland's kitchen.

**Fishing and floating (Episode 3).** The Henry's Fork runs right through St. Anthony. Harriman State Park, Box Canyon, and Last Chance are famous fly-fishing water. The South Fork of the Snake offers guided floats and fishing. Fishing days can be half days or full days, and they work well when the anglers split off from the group.

**Lava country (Episode 4).** Close to home: the St. Anthony Sand Dunes and Menan Buttes. A half day: Mesa Falls on the Mesa Falls Scenic Byway, paired with Warm River. A long day: Craters of the Moon, with the EBR-I Atomic Museum on the way.

**Rainy days (Episode 6).** The game room and media room at The Burland, the Teton Flood Museum in Rexburg, the Museum of Idaho in Idaho Falls, the hot springs, and in summer, Yellowstone Bear World and the Porter Park carousel.

**The Tetons and Jackson (Episode 7).** A long day through Teton Valley and over Teton Pass to Grand Teton National Park, Jenny Lake, Mormon Row, and Jackson.

**Close to home, any time.** The Henry's Fork Greenway in St. Anthony for an evening walk. In summer, the Sand Bar, a sandy swimming spot on the Henry's Fork just east of Keefer Park. The Ashton-Tetonia Trail, a rail trail with old trestle bridges and Teton views, for a family bike ride.

## Sample week layouts

These are examples, not rules. Every family is different.

### The classic seven-day week

- **Day 1, arrival:** Families arrive, settle into their suites, and gather for a welcome dinner. The kids find the game room.
- **Day 2, close to home:** St. Anthony Sand Dunes in the morning while the sand is cool. Picnic at Warm River. Mesa Falls in the afternoon. Dinner at The Burland.
- **Day 3, big day:** Yellowstone. Leave early with packed lunches.
- **Day 4, rest day:** Slow morning. Game room tournament. Anglers head to the Henry's Fork. Swimming at the Sand Bar in the afternoon. Big family dinner in the ballroom.
- **Day 5, big day:** The Tetons and Jackson, or Craters of the Moon, depending on the group.
- **Day 6, easy day:** Menan Buttes for the hikers, Rexburg and the Porter Park carousel for the little kids, the hot springs in the evening. The reunion dinner, programs, and the family photo.
- **Day 7, departure:** One more breakfast together, and the long goodbye.

### The shorter long weekend

For a three- or four-night stay:

- **Arrival evening:** Welcome dinner and the game room.
- **Day 2:** Pick one big day: Yellowstone for first-timers, the Tetons or Craters for families who have done Yellowstone before.
- **Day 3:** Close to home: the dunes, the river, or Mesa Falls. The big dinner that evening.
- **Departure morning:** Brunch, photos, goodbyes.

### The low-key week for grandparents and little kids

- Only one big day, Yellowstone, with lots of boardwalk stops.
- Short outings every other day: Yellowstone Bear World (check that it is open for the season), the carousel, Mesa Falls with its boardwalk, Warm River.
- Afternoons back at The Burland for naps and quiet time.
- Evenings together: dinners, movie night in the media room, games.

## Meals for a big group

Feeding 30 or 40 people for a week is the biggest logistics job of a reunion. The Burland's full kitchen is set up for big-group cooking, with a large island, a stainless steel prep kitchen, a coffee bar, and a dining area with seating for a crowd. The Burland does not provide catering, but families are welcome to cook together, do a potluck, or bring in a caterer for a big night.

Ideas that work:

- **Assign each family one dinner.** Each family plans, shops for, and cooks one night. Everyone else gets the night off.
- **Make breakfast simple.** A self-serve breakfast and the coffee bar let people wake up at different times.
- **Pack lunches the night before big days.** Food inside Yellowstone is limited and spread out. A lunch assembly line in the kitchen the night before saves time and money.
- **Consider catering the big night.** Some families bring in a caterer for the main reunion dinner so nobody is stuck in the kitchen during the program and photos.
- **Shop in Rexburg.** Rexburg, a short drive away, has groceries and restaurants for a big stock-up at the start of the week.

## Mixed ages

A reunion week has to work for a lot of different people at once.

**Grandparents.** Choose places with boardwalks and short walks: the Yellowstone geyser basins, Upper Mesa Falls, the Jenny Lake shoreline. Build in rest days. Make sure there is a comfortable place to sit at every stop. The Burland is designed for all ages, and its wedding spaces are ADA accessible. Families with specific accessibility needs should ask The Burland directly.

**Little kids.** Keep big days short where possible, and plan around naps. The Sand Dunes, Warm River, Yellowstone Bear World, and the Porter Park carousel are reliable hits. The game room and the media room are there for downtime.

**Teens.** Give them something that is theirs: sandboarding or riding at the dunes, a hike to the Menan Buttes crater rim or Inspiration Point, a float trip, or a day on the river with the anglers. The game room and the arcade corner are their territory in the evenings.

## Splitting up and coming back together

Not everyone has to do everything. Some of the best reunion weeks split the group on purpose:

- The anglers fish the Henry's Fork while the little kids go to the dunes.
- The hikers climb Menan Buttes while the grandparents take a slow morning at The Burland.
- The teens go sandboarding while the adults visit Rexburg.

Then everyone comes back together for dinner. The Burland's ballroom, with long tables for the whole group, is where the day's stories get told. A shared dinner each night is what turns a week of separate outings into a reunion.

## Practical tips for the planner

- **Plan the big days first.** Put Yellowstone and any other long days on the calendar, then fill in the rest around them.
- **Leave early on big days.** Summer crowds at Yellowstone's entrance and at Jenny Lake build by mid-morning.
- **Check seasons and conditions.** Many places in this region are seasonal. Check current dates, fees, and road conditions before the trip, and again the day before.
- **Book outfitters ahead.** Guided fishing trips, float trips, and rentals fill up in summer.
- **Leave white space.** An unplanned afternoon is not a wasted afternoon at a reunion.

---

## A note on what guests expect

Most families come to a reunion week with a long list of places they want to see. The families who enjoy the week most are usually the ones who pick a few big days and leave room for everything else. The goal is not to see everything in Eastern Idaho. It is to spend a week together, with a few great days out and a lot of good evenings around the same table.

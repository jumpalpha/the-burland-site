## The Henry's Fork of the Snake River

The Henry's Fork is one of the most famous trout streams in North America. It is a spring-fed river, cold and clear year-round, that runs from its source at Big Springs near Island Park south through Harriman State Park and Ashton before joining the main Snake River near St. Anthony.

What makes it exceptional: the water is exceptionally clear and cold, the insect hatches are prolific and predictable, and the rainbow trout grow large. Fly fishing guides travel from across the country to fish the Henry's Fork.

### Big Springs

Big Springs, north of Island Park, is where the Henry's Fork begins. At this point the river wells up from an underground spring and immediately runs full and clear. The fish here are protected -- this is a catch-and-release, artificial-lure-only section -- but the spring and the fish are viewable from a boardwalk. It is worth a stop for the whole family. Kids love watching the large trout suspended in the current below the boardwalk.

### Harriman State Park and the Railroad Ranch water

Harriman State Park was once the Railroad Ranch, a private retreat for wealthy industrialists. The park preserves the historic ranch buildings and miles of the Henry's Fork through open meadow country. Trumpeter swans, sandhill cranes, and moose are common sightings.

The fishing water through Harriman is considered among the best dry-fly water in the world. It is also among the most technical. The fish are large, wild, and educated -- they have seen every fly pattern. Experienced fly fishers come here specifically for the challenge. For a beginner, the Harriman water is beautiful but humbling.

The park requires a fishing permit for the ranch water, in addition to an Idaho fishing license. Check current permit requirements with Idaho Fish and Game and Idaho State Parks before visiting.

### Box Canyon

Just downstream from Harriman, the Henry's Fork drops through Box Canyon. The canyon section is fast, deep pocket water with big trout. It is wading water for experienced anglers -- the current is strong and the footing is uneven. Beginners should stay out of the canyon itself, but the canyon rim trail offers a dramatic walk with views of the river below.

### Last Chance

The small community of Last Chance, on the edge of the park near Macks Inn, is the hub of Henry's Fork fly fishing. Local fly shops here are staffed by working guides who fish the river daily. For a family visit, stopping at a Last Chance fly shop is worth the trip:

- They know exactly what is hatching and what patterns are working.
- They offer guided fishing trips, half-day and full-day, wade fishing and float.
- They rent gear.
- They are friendly to beginners and can point a first-timer to the right water.

A guided half-day trip through a Last Chance outfitter is the best way for a beginner to have a good first experience on the Henry's Fork. The guide does the rigging, reads the water, and nets the fish. A beginner can catch a 20-inch Henry's Fork rainbow on their first fishing trip with the right guide.

### Where beginners can catch something

The Henry's Fork above Ashton, in the stretch between Last Chance and the Vernon Bridge area, has sections with easier access and less technical fishing than Harriman. Beginners do better in this water than in the Railroad Ranch section.

The Warm River area, where the Warm River joins the Henry's Fork south of Ashton, is a good beginner spot. The fish are smaller but catchable, the access is easy, and the river is beautiful. This is a good stop after Mesa Falls.

## The South Fork of the Snake River

The South Fork of the Snake runs through a dramatic canyon south of Rexburg, from Palisades Reservoir in Swan Valley downstream through the canyon to below Heise and on to Idaho Falls. The canyon float is one of the classic family outdoor experiences in Eastern Idaho.

### Floating the South Fork

The South Fork is a float-fishing river. The canyon section from the Palisades area down through Swan Valley is the stretch most outfitters run. Drifting the canyon in a raft or drift boat with a guide is the way most families experience it.

What to expect on a guided float:
- Full-day trips run six to ten miles of river.
- The canyon walls rise hundreds of feet above the water. This is big, dramatic scenery.
- Bald eagles nest in the canyon and are commonly seen. Great blue herons, ospreys, and mergansers are regular sightings.
- The fishing is for native cutthroat trout and large rainbow trout.
- The floating is gentle -- no whitewater. It is accessible for older adults and kids.

Outfitters in Swan Valley run guided float trips, supplying all equipment including rods, waders, and flies. No prior fishing experience is needed. For a family reunion group, a float trip on the South Fork is one of the best full-day options in the region.

### Non-fishers on the float

A South Fork float trip is enjoyable even for people who do not fish. The canyon scenery, the wildlife, and the experience of drifting a river through remote country are worth the trip. Many outfitters accommodate non-fishing passengers in the raft. The guide does the work; passengers can fish, watch, or just float.

## Other nearby fishing

### Island Park Reservoir

Island Park Reservoir is a large, impounded lake north of Harriman. It is primarily a boat-fishing lake for rainbow and brown trout. It has a boat launch and campground. For a family with a boat or willingness to rent one, the reservoir offers a more relaxed day than the technical river fishing. Ice fishing on the reservoir in winter is popular.

### Henry's Lake State Park

Henry's Lake, in the high country near the Montana border, is known for trophy-sized trout -- hybrid cutthroat-rainbow crosses that reach weights unusual for this region. It is primarily a boat-fishing lake. The state park has a boat launch and campground. The lake's reputation draws serious trophy hunters. The drive goes through scenic high-elevation country near the Yellowstone border.

### Warm River

The Warm River, a tributary of the Henry's Fork south of Ashton, is fed by warm springs and runs clear year-round. It is a good beginner spot: smaller fish, easier access, and forgiving conditions. The fish-feeding spot at the Warm River campground, where large trout congregate near the bridge, is a classic stop for kids and families. The fish are protected here and can be fed but not caught.

## Licenses and regulations

Fishing in Idaho requires a valid Idaho fishing license for anyone 14 and older. Licenses are available online through Idaho Fish and Game, at sporting goods stores, and at fly shops in Last Chance and Island Park. Non-residents can buy short-term licenses by the day or for the season.

The Henry's Fork has sections with special regulations beyond the standard license:
- Some sections require artificial lures only.
- Some sections have catch-and-release requirements.
- Some sections have special permit requirements.

These regulations change and vary by section. The definitive source is the current Idaho Fish and Game fishing regulations booklet, available free at license vendors and online. Never fish a section without checking its current rules.

The South Fork of the Snake has its own regulations for the canyon section. Again: check Idaho Fish and Game before fishing.

## What a beginner needs

For a beginner joining the family for a day of fishing, the simplest path is a guided trip. The guide supplies everything and teaches as you go.

For a beginner fishing on their own, the basics:
- An Idaho fishing license (required age 14 and up)
- A simple spinning rod and reel setup (not fly fishing) -- less technique required, still effective
- A handful of small spinners or PowerBait, appropriate to the river
- A landing net

Fly fishing is the traditional method on the Henry's Fork, but it takes time to learn. Spinning gear catches fish on the Henry's Fork and is a better fit for a beginner's first day.

For a family wanting to try fly fishing, a half-day lesson with a guide is the right introduction. Most Last Chance outfitters offer beginner lessons combined with a guided session on the water.

## Building a family fishing day

The challenge for reunion groups is that fishing enthusiasm varies widely. Here is a framework that works:

**Option A: Guided float trip, South Fork.** Book a full-day guided float from a Swan Valley outfitter. Fishing and non-fishing family members both come. The guide runs the trip; non-fishers enjoy the canyon. This is the easiest option to organize and the most reliable for a good experience regardless of fishing skill.

**Option B: Mesa Falls and Warm River with beginner fishing.** Morning at Mesa Falls, lunch at Warm River, afternoon fishing at Warm River and the lower Henry's Fork near Ashton. Relaxed, scenic, and appropriate for all ages. Fish size is smaller but catching something is realistic.

**Option C: Last Chance and the Henry's Fork for the anglers.** Drop serious fly fishers at Last Chance or the Railroad Ranch access for the morning while the rest of the family visits Big Springs, the Macks Inn area, or Harriman's ranch trail. Regroup for lunch at a Last Chance restaurant, then afternoon activities together.

## A note on fishing culture

The fly fishing culture around the Henry's Fork is serious and courteous. Anglers give each other space. Wading through someone else's water is poor form. For a family group, staying aware of other anglers on the water keeps the day pleasant for everyone.

The river also has a conservation legacy. The Henry's Fork Foundation has worked for decades to maintain and restore the fishery. The catch-and-release culture on much of the river exists because wild trout populations are fragile and worth protecting. For a family introducing kids to fishing, the idea of catching and releasing -- that the fish goes back so someone else can catch it tomorrow -- is worth talking about.

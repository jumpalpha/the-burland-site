## About this episode

Episode 9 is about the small towns of Eastern Idaho and the edge of Montana, and the food this region is known for. Big parks get the attention, but a reunion week also happens in between: a grocery run, a lunch stop, a walk around a small downtown, a museum on a slow afternoon. This episode introduces the towns families will pass through and the local flavors worth looking for.

The episode covers:
1. St. Anthony: The Burland's hometown
2. Rexburg: the college town next door
3. Ashton: seed potatoes and a famous sled dog race
4. Island Park: the town with the long main street
5. West Yellowstone: the railroad gateway to the park
6. Driggs and Victor: Teton Valley on the Idaho side
7. Local food: potatoes, huckleberries, trout, and finger steaks
8. How to work small towns into a reunion week

## When to go: the towns are there all year

This episode is released in late winter. Small towns do not close for the season, and every town in this episode can be part of a trip at any time of year. But some of what is in and around them is seasonal:

- **St. Anthony's Pioneer Days** happens in late July.
- **The Porter Park carousel in Rexburg** runs seasonally, generally early summer through Labor Day.
- **The Museum of the Yellowstone in West Yellowstone** is open seasonally.
- **The American Dog Derby in Ashton** is a winter event.
- **Wild huckleberries** ripen in summer, mostly from midsummer into August depending on elevation.

Always tell listeners to check current dates and hours before they go.

## About The Burland (brief reminder)

The Burland is a restored 1912 building in downtown St. Anthony, Idaho, used for family reunions, weddings, and group stays. It sleeps 40 or more people overnight in private family suites and has a full kitchen set up for big-group cooking. The building went up in 1912, during the years after the railroad reached town and downtown St. Anthony boomed. This episode starts right outside its front door.

## St. Anthony

St. Anthony is a small town on the Henry's Fork of the Snake River, and the county seat of Fremont County.

**A short history.** Around 1890, C.H. Moon settled here and named the town after St. Anthony Falls in Minnesota. Fremont County was created in 1893, with St. Anthony as the county seat. The railroad reached town in 1899, and downtown boomed. The Burland building went up downtown in 1912.

**The river.** The Henry's Fork, one of the West's famous trout rivers, runs right through town. The paved Henry's Fork Greenway follows the river near Bridge Street and makes an easy evening walk. In winter, when the snow is good, it doubles as a cross-country ski trail. In summer, locals cool off at the Sand Bar, a sandy swimming spot on the river just east of Keefer Park.

**Pioneer Days.** St. Anthony's Pioneer Days celebration happens in late July, and it is one of the busiest times of the summer in town.

**The Sand Dunes.** Just west of town, the St. Anthony Sand Dunes cover about 10,600 acres of white quartz sand managed by the Bureau of Land Management, with some dunes rising around 400 feet.

## Rexburg

Rexburg is the college town next door and the place most Burland guests go for groceries and supplies.

**History.** Rexburg was founded in 1883 by Latter-day Saint settlers led by Thomas E. Ricks. The town's name grew out of his family name. Rexburg became the county seat of Madison County when that county was created in 1913.

**BYU-Idaho.** Rexburg is home to Brigham Young University-Idaho. The school was long known as Ricks College, named for the town's founder, and became BYU-Idaho in 2001.

**Temples.** Both Rexburg temples are a short drive from The Burland. Some families plan a temple sealing in the morning and a celebration at The Burland in the afternoon.

**The Teton Flood story.** Rexburg was flooded when the Teton Dam failed on June 5, 1976. The Museum of Rexburg tells that story in its Teton Flood exhibit. Call ahead for hours.

**Porter Park and the carousel.** Porter Park is a big, shady city park with the Idaho Centennial Carousel, an antique wooden carousel built in 1926. It was damaged in the 1976 flood and restored around Idaho's centennial in 1990. It runs seasonally in summer.

## Ashton

Ashton is a small farm town north of St. Anthony on US-20, at the start of the Mesa Falls Scenic Byway.

**Seed potatoes.** The farm country around Ashton is known as one of Idaho's major certified seed potato growing areas. Seed potatoes are the potatoes farmers plant to grow the next crop, and they have to be free of disease, so where they are grown matters. In late summer and fall, the fields around Ashton are part of the potato harvest that shapes life across Eastern Idaho.

**The American Dog Derby.** Ashton is home to the American Dog Derby, a sled dog race first run in 1917. It is often described as the oldest sled dog race in the lower 48 states. The race took a long break in the twentieth century and was revived in 1993. It is a winter event, so check its schedule if the family is visiting in winter.

**Gateway to Mesa Falls.** Ashton is where the Mesa Falls Scenic Byway begins. In summer, a loop from Ashton past Warm River and Upper and Lower Mesa Falls makes a scenic half-day trip. The Ashton-Tetonia Trail, a rail trail with old trestle bridges, also starts near Ashton.

## Island Park

Island Park is not a typical town. It is a long, narrow city stretched along US-20 through the forest north of Ashton, lined with lodges, cabins, and businesses.

**The long main street.** Island Park was incorporated in 1947. At the time, Idaho allowed liquor licenses only for businesses inside an incorporated city, so the lodge owners along the highway drew the city limits to take in their businesses. The result is a city that is long and very narrow. Island Park calls its stretch of US-20 the "longest main street in America."

**Big Springs.** Near Island Park, Big Springs is where the Henry's Fork begins, welling up out of the volcanic rock. Next to the springs is the Johnny Sack Cabin, a hand-built log cabin from the early twentieth century that is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.

**Harriman State Park.** The old Harriman family Railroad Ranch, donated to Idaho in 1965, is now a state park inside an 11,000-acre wildlife refuge, with historic ranch buildings, trumpeter swans, moose, and elk.

Island Park is a summer playground for fishing, boating, and cabins, and a winter destination for snowmobiling.

## West Yellowstone

West Yellowstone, Montana, sits at Yellowstone's West Entrance. It is the closest full-service gateway to the park from The Burland, with gas, food, and lodging.

**A railroad town.** West Yellowstone grew up around the railroad. The first passenger train arrived in 1908. The Union Pacific's stone depot, completed in 1909, still stands and now houses the Museum of the Yellowstone, which tells the story of travel to Yellowstone and of the town itself. The museum is open seasonally. Passenger trains stopped running to West Yellowstone in 1960, as more and more visitors arrived by car.

**Wildlife up close.** The Grizzly and Wolf Discovery Center, a nonprofit wildlife park near the park entrance, is open every day of the year.

**A winter town too.** In winter, West Yellowstone becomes a base for snowmobiling and for guided snowmobile and snowcoach trips into Yellowstone.

## Driggs and Victor

Driggs and Victor are small towns in Teton Valley, on the Idaho side of the Teton Range.

**Teton Valley.** Teton Valley is wide farm country with the Tetons rising along its eastern edge. Families heading to Grand Teton National Park and Jackson pass through here on the way to Teton Pass. Driggs is also the gateway to Grand Targhee Resort.

**The Spud Drive-In.** Just south of Driggs, the Spud Drive-In is a drive-in movie theater built in the early 1950s and listed on the National Register of Historic Places. It is a well-known local landmark, with a nod to the potato in its name. Check whether it is showing films before planning a visit.

For a family that wants mountain views without the crowds of Jackson, a slow day in Teton Valley is a good alternative.

## Local food

This episode does not recommend specific restaurants. Instead, here are the foods this region is known for, and what to look for on menus and at grocery stores.

**Potatoes.** Idaho is potato country, and Eastern Idaho is at the heart of it. The fields around Ashton and across the valley grow both seed potatoes and the crop itself. In some rural Eastern Idaho school districts, the fall potato harvest has long been important enough that schools have taken a "spud break," a pause of a week or two so students can help bring in the crop. For a reunion, potatoes are the easiest way to feed a crowd: a baked potato bar in The Burland's kitchen is a classic.

**Huckleberries.** The huckleberry is Idaho's official state fruit, designated in 2000. Wild huckleberries grow in the mountains and ripen in summer. Families can pick them for personal use on national forest land, including the Caribou-Targhee, but commercial picking for sale is not allowed there, and pickers should never rake or cut the bushes. Huckleberry jams, syrups, candies, and desserts show up all over the region. One family reunion group that stayed at The Burland mentioned picking huckleberries nearby as a highlight of their trip.

**Trout.** The Henry's Fork and the South Fork of the Snake are famous trout rivers. Anglers should check Idaho Fish and Game rules on which waters allow keeping fish and which are catch-and-release.

**Finger steaks.** Finger steaks, strips of beef that are battered and deep-fried and usually served with a dipping sauce, are an Idaho specialty. The dish is usually traced back to Boise in the 1950s. They show up on menus across the state.

## Building a small-town day

**Option A: Hometown morning.** Walk the Henry's Fork Greenway in St. Anthony. In summer, finish at the Sand Bar for a swim. Lunch back at The Burland.

**Option B: Rexburg afternoon.** Groceries and supplies, the Teton Flood exhibit (call ahead), and in summer, the Porter Park carousel for the little kids.

**Option C: The Ashton and Mesa Falls loop (summer).** North to Ashton, then the Mesa Falls Scenic Byway past Warm River and both falls. A half-day trip with a small farm town at the start and a waterfall at the end.

**Option D: West Yellowstone, beyond the park.** On a Yellowstone day, or on its own, spend time in West Yellowstone: the Museum of the Yellowstone in season and the Grizzly and Wolf Discovery Center.

**Option E: Teton Valley.** A slow day in Driggs and Victor with big views of the Tetons, and Grand Targhee nearby.

## A note on what guests expect

Many families arrive thinking of Eastern Idaho as a place to pass through on the way to Yellowstone. The small towns surprise them. St. Anthony has its own river, its own history, and a 1912 downtown building at the center of it. Ashton has a sled dog race that goes back to 1917. Island Park has a main street that stretches a long way through the forest. These towns are where a reunion week slows down, and where families get a sense of the place they are staying in, not just the parks around it.

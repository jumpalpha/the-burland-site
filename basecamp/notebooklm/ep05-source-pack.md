## About this episode

Episode 5 is about winter. Most families picture Eastern Idaho in summer, with Yellowstone geysers and river days. But winter here is its own season, with snowmobile country that draws riders from across the West, a ski resort on the quiet side of the Tetons, a small local ski hill, hot springs, and Yellowstone itself in its snowbound off-season. This episode tells listeners how to put together a winter gathering, from a long holiday weekend to a snowmobile trip.

The episode covers:
1. Why winter in Eastern Idaho is worth a trip
2. Snowmobile country: Island Park, the Mesa Falls Scenic Byway as a winter route, and West Yellowstone
3. Yellowstone in winter: guided snowmobile and snowcoach trips
4. Skiing: Grand Targhee and Kelly Canyon
5. Easier winter days: cross-country skiing, sleigh rides, hot springs, and snow play
6. Holiday gatherings at The Burland
7. How to build a winter day that works for a family group

## When to go: this winter

This episode is released in early winter. The snow season in Eastern Idaho generally runs from December through March, and most of what is in this episode is open now or opens soon.

- **Yellowstone oversnow season:** Park roads generally switch to snowmobile and snowcoach travel only by mid-December, and that season ends in mid-March when plowing begins. Exact dates change each year, so check the National Park Service before planning.
- **Snowmobile trails around Island Park:** groomed through the winter, depending on snow.
- **Ski areas:** open dates depend on snow. Check each resort before you go.
- **National Elk Refuge sleigh rides in Jackson:** generally start in mid to late December and run into early April, weather permitting.
- **St. Anthony Sand Dunes:** the big dunes close to all use from January 1 to April 1 to protect wintering elk, mule deer, and moose. Do not plan winter dune trips after New Year's.

Always tell listeners to check current conditions and seasons before they go.

## About The Burland (brief reminder)

The Burland is a restored 1912 building in downtown St. Anthony, Idaho, used for family reunions, weddings, and group stays. It sleeps 40 or more people overnight in private family suites, so most families fit under one roof. Winter is when The Burland feels coziest: snow outside, everyone inside, and plenty of room to spread out.

## Why winter here is worth a trip

St. Anthony sits at the doorstep of some of the best snow country in the Northern Rockies. To the north, Island Park has one of the largest networks of groomed snowmobile trails in Idaho. To the east, across Teton Valley, Grand Targhee Resort sits on the west side of the Teton Range and is known for heavy, reliable snowfall. Yellowstone's West Entrance, in winter, is a starting point for guided snowmobile and snowcoach trips into the park.

Winter also changes the feel of a family gathering. In summer, groups scatter to different day trips. In winter, the days are shorter, and families tend to come back to one warm place in the evening. A winter reunion is often more about the time together indoors, with a few big outdoor days mixed in.

## Snowmobile country

### Island Park

Island Park, north of Ashton along US-20, is the heart of snowmobiling in Eastern Idaho. It has one of Idaho's largest groomed snowmobile trail networks. Rentals and guided trips run out of Island Park, so families do not need to bring their own machines. Two Top is one of the best-known riding areas.

Island Park in winter is quiet country: forest, open meadows, frozen reservoirs, and long groomed trails. A guided trip is the best choice for first-timers. Guides know the trails, the weather, and the terrain.

### The Mesa Falls Scenic Byway as a winter route

In summer, the Mesa Falls Scenic Byway is a paved road from Ashton through the Caribou-Targhee National Forest to Upper and Lower Mesa Falls. In winter, it changes. The road is plowed only partway, to the Bear Gulch area northeast of Ashton, where a parking lot serves as a trailhead. Beyond that point, the byway becomes a snowmobile and ski route rather than a road for cars.

That means Mesa Falls in winter is reachable only by snowmobile or skis. For families who make the trip, it is worth it: the falls become a frozen wonder, with snow and ice along the canyon and a quiet forest all around. Upper Mesa Falls drops about 114 feet. In winter, it is one of the most striking sights in the region, and very few people see it.

### West Yellowstone

West Yellowstone, Montana, sits at Yellowstone's West Entrance. In winter it is a snowmobile town. Riders use it as a base for trails outside the park and for guided trips into Yellowstone itself. Rentals and outfitters are plentiful.

### Practical notes for snowmobiling

- Out-of-state riders bringing their own snowmobiles to Idaho need an Idaho nonresident snowmobile user certificate. If you rent, ask the outfitter what is covered. Check Idaho Parks and Recreation for current rules.
- Dress in layers, with a helmet, goggles, insulated gloves, and boots. Outfitters often rent winter gear along with the machines.
- Check avalanche and weather conditions before going anywhere off groomed trails. Ride with others. Consider a guide if anyone in the group is new to it.

## Yellowstone in winter

Yellowstone in winter is a different park. Most roads close to regular cars, and travel inside the park is by snowmobile or snowcoach. A snowcoach is an oversnow vehicle built for groups, with heat and big windows. It is a good option for grandparents, younger kids, or anyone who does not want to drive a snowmobile.

Most winter visitors go with a commercial guide. Guided snowmobile tours and snowcoach tours leave from West Yellowstone, the closest entrance to St. Anthony, and head to places like Old Faithful and the geyser basins. There is also a limited permit program for riders who want to go without a commercial guide, with only a few groups allowed in each day, and permit holders must complete training first. Most families will find a guided trip much simpler.

What makes winter in Yellowstone special: steam rising from the geyser basins in the cold, bison in the snow, and far fewer people than in summer. It is a long day from The Burland, and it needs to be booked ahead with an outfitter.

## Skiing

### Grand Targhee Resort

Grand Targhee sits on the west side of the Teton Range in Alta, Wyoming, reached through Driggs, Idaho. It is known for deep, frequent powder and for being on the quieter side of the Tetons compared with the resorts near Jackson. For a family group with skiers and snowboarders, it is a full-day trip from The Burland.

### Kelly Canyon Ski Area

Kelly Canyon, near Ririe, is a small, local ski hill with about 26 runs. It offers night skiing on a schedule that changes by season. It is a good choice for a first ski day, for younger kids, or for a group that wants a relaxed day on the snow rather than a big-mountain day. Heise Hot Springs is nearby, and a hot soak after skiing at Kelly Canyon is a local favorite.

## Easier winter days

Not everyone in a reunion group wants to ride a snowmobile or ski all day. These options are easier and work for mixed ages.

**Cross-country skiing in town.** In St. Anthony, the Henry's Fork Greenway along the river doubles as a cross-country ski trail when the snow is good. It is right in town.

**Harriman State Park.** Harriman, in Island Park, is a well-known winter Nordic destination. It has groomed trails for classic and skate skiing, snowshoeing, and fat biking, and the park rents yurts for overnight stays. The Henry's Fork through Harriman is also a major wintering area for trumpeter swans. Seeing them on open water in the snow is something people remember. The park charges fees, so check Idaho Parks and Recreation for current conditions and costs.

**Sleigh rides on the National Elk Refuge.** In Jackson, Wyoming, horse-drawn sleigh rides take visitors out to see the elk that spend the winter on the National Elk Refuge. Rides generally run from mid to late December into early April, weather permitting. It is a long day from The Burland, so pair it with time in Jackson.

**Hot springs.** Heise Hot Springs, near Ririe, keeps its hot pools open year-round. Its summer pool and waterslide are summer only. Green Canyon Hot Springs, a family-run hot pool in a quiet canyon, has more limited hours in the off-season. Call ahead for either.

**Grizzly and Wolf Discovery Center.** In West Yellowstone, this nonprofit wildlife park is open every day of the year. Its grizzly bears do not hibernate, so winter is a good time to see them active in the snow.

**Snow play.** Sledding, building snow forts, and snowball fights need no planning. Ask locally for a safe sledding hill, and remember that the St. Anthony Sand Dunes close from January 1 to April 1 to protect wintering wildlife.

## Holiday gatherings at The Burland

Winter holidays are when many families most want to be together, and The Burland is built for a big group under one roof.

- **Room for everyone:** The Burland sleeps 40 or more in private family suites, with suite living rooms and kitchenettes, so each family has its own space.
- **The big dinner:** The ballroom has crystal chandeliers, long dinner tables, and a stacked firewood feature wall. It seats 100. This is where the holiday meal happens.
- **Cooking together:** The full kitchen is set up for big-group cooking, with a large island and a stainless steel prep kitchen. Families cook together, do a potluck, or bring a caterer. The Burland does not provide catering.
- **Kids on a snow day:** The game room has ping-pong, air hockey, and an arcade corner. The media room is set up for movie night.
- **Somewhere to sit and talk:** The upstairs lounge has sectionals overlooking the ballroom, and there is a coffee bar.

Ask The Burland directly about holiday dates.

## Building a winter day

**Option A: Snowmobile day.** Rent machines or book a guide in Island Park and spend the day on groomed trails. Strong riders with a guide can make the winter trip to Mesa Falls. Back to The Burland for dinner.

**Option B: Yellowstone by snowcoach.** Book ahead with an outfitter in West Yellowstone. Leave early for a full day in the park, with Old Faithful and the geyser basins in the snow. Stop at the Grizzly and Wolf Discovery Center if there is time. A long day, best for groups who have booked well in advance.

**Option C: Ski and soak.** Skiers spend the day at Grand Targhee or Kelly Canyon. Non-skiers can spend the morning cross-country skiing on the Henry's Fork Greenway or at Harriman. Finish at Heise Hot Springs, then dinner together at The Burland.

**Option D: The easy snow day.** Stay close to home. Sledding in the morning, the game room and a big pot of soup at lunch, movie night in the media room. Grandparents and little kids will not mind at all.

## A note on what guests expect

Most guests think of Eastern Idaho as a summer place. Winter surprises them. Snowmobilers already know Island Park, but families are often amazed that Yellowstone is open in winter, that Mesa Falls freezes into something they have never seen, and that a frozen elk refuge has sleigh rides. Winter gatherings also tend to be closer and calmer. With short days and snow outside, families spend more time together around one table. For many groups, that is the whole point of a reunion.

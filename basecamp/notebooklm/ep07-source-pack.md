## About this episode

Episode 7 is about the other famous mountains on the horizon: the Teton Range and Grand Teton National Park, with the town of Jackson, Wyoming, at the south end of the valley. Many families staying at The Burland plan a Yellowstone day. Fewer realize that the Tetons are also within reach for a full day trip, on a route that crosses Teton Valley on the Idaho side. ## When to go: plan it for summer

This episode is released in the middle of winter, when the Tetons are deep in snow. That makes it a planning episode. Summer is the season for this day trip.

- **Teton Park Road:** the main road along the base of the mountains, past Jenny Lake, is closed to cars from November 1 through April 30 between Taggart Lake Trailhead and Signal Mountain Lodge. It generally opens to cars on May 1, depending on weather.
- **Jenny Lake boat shuttle:** seasonal, generally running from late spring or early summer into early fall. Start dates have changed in past years, so check before you go.
- **Visitor centers, lodges, and campgrounds:** many are seasonal and begin closing in September.
- **Jackson Hole Aerial Tram:** runs for sightseeing in summer, roughly late spring into early fall.
- **Teton Pass:** a steep mountain highway that can close or slow for weather or slides. Check current road conditions from the Wyoming Department of Transportation before you go.

Always tell listeners to check current conditions and seasons before they go.

## About The Burland (brief reminder)

The Burland is a restored 1912 building in downtown St. Anthony, Idaho, used for family reunions, weddings, and group stays. It sleeps 40 or more people overnight in private family suites and has a full kitchen set up for big-group cooking, which makes it easy to pack lunches for a long day out. This episode is about the big mountains to the southeast.

## The Teton Range

The Tetons rise straight up from the valley floor with almost no foothills, which is why they look so dramatic. The reason is the Teton fault, which runs along the base of the range. Over millions of years, movement along the fault has lifted the mountains up on one side and dropped the valley floor down on the other. The National Park Service notes that the fault began lifting the range less than ten million years ago, which makes the Tetons one of the youngest mountain ranges in the world.

The highest peak, the Grand Teton, rises to 13,775 feet. Grand Teton National Park protects the range and much of the valley below it, called Jackson Hole. "Hole" was a fur trappers' word for a large mountain valley ringed by peaks, and the valley is named for David Jackson, a trapper who worked there in the 1820s.

From St. Anthony, families see the Tetons from the west, the Idaho side. Grand Teton National Park and Jackson are on the east side, in Wyoming.

## Getting there, in general terms

There are two sides to this trip.

**The Idaho side: Teton Valley.** The route from St. Anthony heads southeast into Teton Valley, through the small towns of Driggs and Victor, on the Idaho side of the Tetons. Teton Valley has wide farm country with the mountains rising above it, and it is the gateway to Grand Targhee Resort.

**Over Teton Pass.** From Victor, the highway climbs over Teton Pass into Wyoming and drops down toward Jackson. Teton Pass is a steep mountain road with sharp grades and big views. It is fine for ordinary cars in summer, but drivers should take it slowly, use lower gears on the descent, and check road conditions before going, since it has closed for weather and slides in the past.

From Jackson, the park is just to the north. Many families make a loop: over Teton Pass in the morning, through the park, into Jackson in the afternoon, and back over the pass in the evening.

This is a long day from The Burland. Leave early.

## Jenny Lake

Jenny Lake sits at the foot of the Tetons, and it is the most popular stop in the park. The Jenny Lake Visitor Center and the area around it have trails, views, and a boat dock.

**The boat shuttle.** A seasonal shuttle boat, run by Jenny Lake Boating, carries visitors across the lake to the west shore dock at the base of the mountains. It charges a fare. Taking the boat cuts out most of the walking and puts families right at the trailhead.

**Hidden Falls and Inspiration Point.** From the west shore boat dock, a short hike leads to Hidden Falls, a waterfall tucked into Cascade Canyon. Inspiration Point is farther up, a steeper climb with a big view back across Jenny Lake and the valley. Hidden Falls is the right goal for most mixed-age groups. Inspiration Point is for the stronger hikers.

**For families who skip the hike.** Walking along the Jenny Lake shoreline near the visitor center gives grandparents and young kids the mountain views without the climb.

Parking at Jenny Lake fills up in summer. Arriving early helps.

## The classic views

**Mormon Row.** In the southeast part of the park, along Antelope Flats Road, Mormon Row is a historic district of old homesteads. Latter-day Saint settlers from Idaho first arrived here in the 1890s. Its barns, with the Tetons rising behind them, draw photographers from around the world. The T.A. Moulton barn and the John Moulton barn are the best known. Mormon Row is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. For many reunion families, it is the best place in the park for a group photo.

**Oxbow Bend.** Oxbow Bend is a paved pullout along the Snake River in the northern part of the park, with Mount Moran rising behind the water. On calm days, the mountain reflects in the river. The National Park Service lists moose, otters, beavers, pelicans, herons, ospreys, and bald eagles among the wildlife seen here, though sightings vary. Early morning and evening are the best times.

**Craig Thomas Discovery and Visitor Center.** At Moose, near the park's south entrance, this visitor center has exhibits on the park and is a good first stop for maps, current conditions, and ranger advice. It is seasonal, so check its hours.

Practical note: the park charges an entrance fee, and an America the Beautiful pass covers it. Entrance fees and pass rules have changed recently, so check the National Park Service's current fee page before the trip.

## Jackson

Jackson, Wyoming, is the town at the south end of Jackson Hole. It is busy in summer, with shops, galleries, and restaurants around a central square.

**The antler arches.** Jackson's Town Square has four arches made of elk antlers at its corners. The first was built in 1953, and it took local volunteers, including the Rotary Club and the Boy Scouts, years to finish all four. The antlers are naturally shed by the elk that winter on the nearby National Elk Refuge. Local Scouts collect the sheds each spring. Taking antlers from the arches is illegal. The arches are a classic family photo stop.

**The Jackson Hole Aerial Tram.** At Teton Village, northwest of Jackson, the aerial tram at Jackson Hole Mountain Resort carries visitors to the top of Rendezvous Mountain, at 10,450 feet, for a huge view of the valley and the range. In summer it runs for sightseeing. It requires a ticket and can be windy and cold at the top even in summer, so bring a jacket.

**The National Elk Refuge.** Just north of town, the refuge protects the winter range of a large elk herd. The refuge borders the road between Jackson and the park, so families can take a look on the way in or out.

## Wildlife and safety

Grand Teton National Park is home to bison, elk, moose, pronghorn, black bears, and grizzly bears. Wildlife sightings are a highlight, but they come with rules:

- The National Park Service asks visitors to stay at least 100 yards from bears and wolves and at least 25 yards from all other wildlife, including bison, elk, and moose.
- Use binoculars or a long camera lens instead of getting closer.
- Do not stop in the road to watch wildlife. Use pullouts.
- Carry bear spray on trails and know how to use it.

## Building a Tetons day

**Option A: The classic loop.** Leave very early with packed lunches. Through Teton Valley and over Teton Pass. Into the park: Mormon Row for the family photo, then Jenny Lake. Take the boat across and hike to Hidden Falls. Lunch by the lake. Afternoon in Jackson at the antler arches and the town square. Dinner in Jackson or back at The Burland. A long day, but the one most families want.

**Option B: The easy-pace Tetons day.** For grandparents and little kids. Visitor center at Moose, then a slow drive through the park with stops at the big viewpoints. A short walk along the Jenny Lake shore instead of the boat and hike. Oxbow Bend in the late afternoon for wildlife. Skip Jackson or keep it short.

**Option C: Jackson and the tram.** Over Teton Pass to Jackson in the morning. Ride the aerial tram at Teton Village. Lunch in Jackson, then the town square and antler arches. Add a short stop in the park at Mormon Row on the way home if there is energy left.

**Option D: Stay on the Idaho side.** If a full day to Jackson is too much, spend a day in Teton Valley instead: Driggs and Victor, the Teton views from the west, and Grand Targhee Resort, which has scenic chairlift rides and hiking in summer. This is a shorter day with big mountain views and smaller crowds.

## A note on what guests expect

Many families plan a reunion around Yellowstone and think of the Tetons as a separate trip. From The Burland, both are possible, on different days. The Tetons are a different kind of day from Yellowstone: fewer geysers, more mountains, and a classic western town at the end. The view of the range from Mormon Row or across Jenny Lake is the photo many families frame when they get home. Plan it for summer, leave early, and pack lunches.

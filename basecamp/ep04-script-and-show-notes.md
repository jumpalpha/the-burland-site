## Part 1: Script outline

### 1. Opening (about 1 minute)
- Welcome to Burland Basecamp, the podcast from The Burland, a restored 1912 building in St. Anthony, Idaho.
- Most guests come for Yellowstone and the Tetons and drive right past lava country without knowing it.
- Say up front: this one is a planning episode. Lava country is a late-spring to early-fall trip, and now is the time to pick next year's reunion dates.

### 2. Why Eastern Idaho looks like this (about 2 minutes)
- The Snake River Plain is the track left as the continent moved over the Yellowstone hotspot, the same engine behind Yellowstone's geysers.
- Some of the lava fields are among the youngest on the continent, some as recent as about two thousand years ago.
- The plain is a giant sponge: water soaks into the basalt and feeds the Eastern Snake Plain Aquifer.
- The Henry's Fork starts at Big Springs near Island Park, where water wells up from the volcanic rock.

### 3. Close to home: Menan Buttes and the Sand Dunes (about 3 minutes)
- Menan Buttes: two tuff cones west of Rexburg, near Menan. Steam blasts where magma met water built them from volcanic ash that hardened into tan, layered rock.
- A National Natural Landmark, counted among the largest volcanic tuff cones in the world.
- North Butte: short, steep trail to the rim, loose footing in places, not for strollers. A hidden crater bowl and a view of the plain, the Tetons, and the river.
- St. Anthony Sand Dunes: white sand in the middle of lava and farmland. ATVs, horses, sandboarding, kids running. Egin Lakes beside them for cooling off.
- Parts of the dunes close in winter and early spring for wildlife. Check BLM rules first.

### 4. Mesa Falls (about 2 minutes)
- On the Mesa Falls Scenic Byway between Ashton and Island Park.
- The Henry's Fork drops over the Mesa Falls Tuff, hardened ash from a giant hotspot eruption.
- Upper Mesa Falls about 114 feet, with a boardwalk and Big Falls Inn, a historic lodge used as a summer visitor center. Lower Mesa Falls about 65 feet, seen from an overlook along the byway.
- The byway is closed to cars in winter and becomes a snowmobile route.

### 5. Hell's Half Acre (about 1 minute)
- A lava field south and west of Idaho Falls. Easiest way in: the short paved trail at the I-15 rest area between Idaho Falls and Blackfoot.
- Pahoehoe (ropy and smooth) and aa (sharp and broken). Thousands of years old, not millions.
- A quick stop on a drive, for example to or from the Idaho Falls airport. Not a destination on its own.

### 6. EBR-I Atomic Museum (about 2 minutes)
- On the lava plain southwest of Idaho Falls. On December 20, 1951, the first electricity from nuclear energy was made here.
- Walk through the original building and see the reactor and its control room.
- Free, run by the Department of Energy, open generally Memorial Day through Labor Day.
- On the way to Craters of the Moon.

### 7. Craters of the Moon (about 3 minutes)
- The full-day trip. Cinder cones, lava tubes, spatter cones, and tree molds, across a monument and preserve roughly the size of Rhode Island.
- Inferno Cone: short, steep climb on loose cinders, view of the whole monument.
- Caves: a free cave permit from the visitor center first. Indian Tunnel can be walked without a flashlight. Dewdrop, Boy Scout, and Beauty need a flashlight or headlamp. Closed-toe shoes with grip. Bring a layer.
- Spatter cones and the Tree Molds trail.
- Bring water, there is little shade. The park charges an entry fee, and an America the Beautiful pass covers it.
- The loop road closes to cars in winter and caves can close for the season. Check current conditions.

### 8. Building a lava-country day (about 1 minute)
- Option A, close to home, half day: Sand Dunes in the morning, Menan Buttes in the afternoon, cool off at Egin Lakes.
- Option B, Mesa Falls and the byway: Warm River picnic, Upper and Lower Mesa Falls, back through Ashton.
- Option C, Craters full day: leave early, about 45 minutes at EBR-I (summer only), the rest of the day at Craters. Lunch, water, and closed-toe shoes for everyone.

### 9. Close (under 1 minute)
- For groups that have done Yellowstone before, lava country is often the discovery of the trip.
- Plan it for next spring and summer, and check current conditions before going.
- Coming up: the Tetons, planning the full reunion week, and rainy days in Eastern Idaho.

### Full show notes (for an announcement post or a future episode page)

Most families come to Eastern Idaho for Yellowstone and the Tetons. Right outside The Burland's door is something stranger: some of the youngest lava fields on the continent, volcanic cones you can climb, lava tube caves you can walk into, and the place where nuclear power began.

This is a planning episode. Lava country is at its best from late spring through early fall, and now is a good time to pick next year's reunion dates.

**In this episode**
- Why the Snake River Plain looks the way it does, and how it ties to Yellowstone
- Menan Buttes, a National Natural Landmark, and the St. Anthony Sand Dunes
- Mesa Falls, where the Henry's Fork drops over ash from an ancient eruption
- Hell's Half Acre, a quick walk into a lava flow off I-15
- EBR-I, where electricity was first made from nuclear energy in 1951
- A full day at Craters of the Moon: Inferno Cone, the caves, and the spatter cones
- Three ways to build a lava-country day for a reunion group

**Plan it for spring and summer**
- Craters of the Moon: the loop road closes to cars in winter. A free permit is needed for the caves.
- EBR-I: open generally Memorial Day through Labor Day.
- Mesa Falls Scenic Byway: closed to cars in winter.
- St. Anthony Sand Dunes: parts close in winter and early spring for wildlife.

Check current conditions and fees before you go.

**Before you go:** closed-toe shoes with grip, water, and a flashlight or headlamp for the caves.
